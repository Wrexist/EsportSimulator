## Result and use

Open **Map Studio → Simulation lab → Encounter lab**. Choose one of the five utility examples and **Open encounter example**, then **Run encounter**. The previous lab test downloads as a backup. Inspect **World truth · observer** to see grenade flight and effects; play, step or scrub the timeline. Player views retain their own inventory, blindness and uncertain sound cues, and withhold hidden opponent trajectories and effects.

**Add throw** creates an editable release schedule. Set the thrower, grenade, release time, direction, elevation, power and mode. **Equip planned throws** fills the test inventory within its limits: four grenades per player, at most two flashes and one of each other kind. A rejected release does not consume inventory; a successful release consumes once. Gunfire can be held while testing utility. Removing a throw does not silently remove equipped inventory. Save/Open test and local lab autosave retain the optional utility setup independently of career saves and Map Studio drawings.

Landing XYZ, tolerance and up to eight expected bounce checkpoints are editable. They compare against the simulation; they never force its flight path. **Use replay as model checkpoints** copies computed values for repeatability testing. Diagnostics distinguish draft, checked against model, mismatch, pending and failed. A model match is explicitly still a real-map draft. Source URLs and review notes remain metadata; imports are never auto-certified. The five Mirage examples are original near-feet diagnostic throws, not imported competitive lineups.

## Declared model

`lab-utility-v1` uses original, provisional tuning in map world units. There is no claim of calibrated CS2 physics. Up to eight scheduled throws run within the existing two-player, 1–20 second encounter. Utility tests default to at least 12 seconds in the editor. Effects and released grenades continue through the requested duration after the thrower's death; dead actors cannot move, fire or release subsequent throws.

- **Flight:** 64 ticks/s, four substeps per tick, gravity 320 units/s², base launch speed 500 units/s multiplied by power. Lob speed is 45%; running mode adds a fixed 120-unit horizontal launch boost, not measured player velocity. Seven offset rays approximate a radius-two sweep. Two-sided triangle normals drive reflection, with configurable retention and friction. Missing normals, blocked release space, excess bounces and flight timeout produce diagnostics. This is not a continuous sphere solver or exact grenade collision hull.
- **Fuses:** HE/flash burst at 1.5 seconds, including airborne bursts. Smoke/decoy need fuse expiry and a supported rest. Fire ignites on a sufficiently upward surface after a short minimum flight. Unsupported smoke/decoy trajectories fail after six seconds. A replay that ends before a scheduled throw or detonation reports pending.
- **Smoke:** radius 120, height 128, lifetime eight seconds. Each occupied sample must have an unobstructed physical connection to the burst. This conservatively prevents disconnected room/floor coverage but does not simulate volumetric flow around corners. Vision samples the segment every eight units near the volume. Smoke obstructs sight and flash exposure; physical bullet rays remain separate. The model currently does not blind-fire. Replay cells show a conservative eye-height slice; they are not the full three-dimensional volume.
- **Flash:** range 1,500, maximum 160 ticks of blindness; distance, gaze direction and geometry/smoke occlusion scale exposure. Looking away reduces rather than always eliminates exposure. Blinded actors lose visual contact and cannot fire; reacquisition requires a new reaction window. This is a binary blindness interval, without a partial visual recovery curve.
- **HE:** radius 350, maximum pre-armor damage 98 with squared distance falloff. Three height samples measure exposed body coverage; opaque walls/floors stop those rays. Armor absorbs up to 35% while available. Friendly/self damage is enabled in this two-actor test.
- **Fire:** supported 20-unit cells within radius 100, conservative same-level connectivity, expanding radius at 60 units/s from an initial 20. Occupants receive six unarmored damage per quarter-second for six seconds. It does not traverse stairs, jump gaps or pass through opaque walls. No smoke extinguishing or water/material interactions are modeled yet.
- **Decoy:** half-second gunfire cues for eight seconds. Opaque cover attenuates the 1,200-unit hearing radius. The listener receives a location quantized to 64 units with no enemy identity, and may turn toward it after reacting. Repeated pulses refresh cue expiry without resetting the first reaction time; a two-second reaction regression covers that boundary. This is a diagnostic sound observation, not audible playback or a general acoustic propagation model. Map Studio remains quiet.

Utility events retain the throw ID and flash duration for replay attribution. Tick ordering is scripted movement, scheduled throws and coeval utility effects, perception, both gun decisions, both shot resolutions, then death/snapshot. Utility can prevent a gun decision in the same tick. Gunshots already scheduled together still resolve together. The static mesh and reviewed bound annotations are the physical world; actors do not serve as grenade blast shields.
