Esports Manager: FPS

Steam Workshop & Community Mods

Play with real teams, logos, player names and portraits — or build and publish your own roster mod for everyone to use.

1Play with a Workshop mod

The base game ships with original, fictional teams. Community mods swap in real names, logos and faces — installed only on your own copy.

  1. On the game's Steam Workshop page, find a mod (e.g. a real-teams roster pack) and click Subscribe. Steam downloads it automatically.
  2. Launch the game and open Settings → Community & Workshop.
  3. Under Steam Workshop, press Refresh — your subscribed mods appear in the list.
  4. Click Activate on the mod you want.
  5. Start a New Career. The mod's teams, logos and portraits are now live. (Existing careers keep the data they started with.)
To switch back to the default fictional world, open the same screen and press Deactivate, then start a new career.

2Make your own mod

A mod is a small folder that overlays the game's data by id — it keeps every team's stats, roster and tier, and only replaces the display name, logo, player nickname and portrait.

manifest.json

Name, author, version, and counts. Marks the folder as a game mod.

teams.json / players.json

Overlay entries. Ids must match the game's built-in ids; only names & art paths change.

assets/…

Your logos and portraits, referenced by relative paths from the JSON.

preview.png

A ~512×512 thumbnail shown on the Workshop.

Folder layout:

my-mod/
  manifest.json
  teams.json                                  # display name + logoPath per team id
  players.json                                # nickname + portraitPath per player id
  assets/teams/<slug>/logo.png               # your team logos
  assets/teams/<slug>/players/<nick>.png      # your player portraits
  preview.png
The game includes a packager that turns a data source into a ready-to-upload mod folder (npm run build:mod) and an editor-friendly JSON format. Asset paths must be relative — remote URLs and paths that escape the folder are rejected for safety.

3Publish to the Steam Workshop

Once your mod folder is ready, upload it as a Workshop item.

  1. Make sure Steam is running and you own Esports Manager: FPS.
  2. Add a preview.png (~512×512, ≤ 1 MB) to your mod folder.
  3. Create & upload the item with the included tool: 
    npm run build:mod
npm run workshop:upload -- --title="My Roster Mod" --preview=preview.png
    It prints a Workshop item id — save it.
  4. To update the same item later, reuse that id: 
    npm run workshop:upload -- --item=<id> --changenote="Roster update"
  5. The first time, accept the Steam Workshop Legal Agreement. Your item appears on the game's Workshop within a minute.
Prefer a manual upload? The mod folder is standard Workshop content — you can also publish it with Steam's own Workshop tools. Either way, players subscribe and activate it exactly as in step 1.

★Rules & guidelines

  • Mods are user-created and user-installed. The base game contains no real-world names, logos or likenesses.
  • Only upload content you have the right to distribute. Real team names, logos and player likenesses belong to their owners.
  • No offensive, misleading, or malicious content. Keep asset files to images and the mod's JSON.
  • Credit sources where you can, and keep your mod updated as rosters change.
